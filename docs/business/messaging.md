## Voice

A local business owner writing to another local business — warm, direct, zero corporate gloss. It's lawn games: the email can smile, but never gimmicky, never salesy. No exclamation marks, no puns in the subject line.

## Structure

1. One specific, true observation about *them* — their patio, their event calendar, their summer series, a recent opening (from research or their record; never invented).
2. One or two sentences: who we are (lawn-game rentals, delivered and set up, now serving Maine) and the one offering that fits *their* events — name one or two actual games, not the whole catalog.
3. One low-friction ask: a reply, or 15 minutes. Nothing more.

## Maine-specific rules

- We are new to Maine: say so plainly ("we're expanding into Maine this season") — it reads as honest, and early customers like being first.
- **Never quote prices, packages, or delivery fees to Maine prospects.** Rates there aren't published. If pricing comes up, the answer is a conversation.
- Massachusetts proof points are fair game: two established locations, the event types we serve.

## Never

- Reference alcohol consumption beyond naming the games (Bru Bag, Giant Beer Pong are product names, that's all).
- Claim a date is available or promise capacity.
- Mention a competitor, or another venue's event, by name as a comparison.
- Reference anything personal about the contact — only their business.

## Footer

The transactional template adds the legal footer and unsubscribe link — do not write one, and sign off with a first name only.
